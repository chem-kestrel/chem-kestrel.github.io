KESTREL

Kwik Electronic Structure for TRansition ELements

A kestrel is a small, fast, and agile falcon, renowned for its ability to hover with precision while searching for prey. Likewise, KESTREL is designed for rapid and precise exploration of the electronic structure, magnetism and spectra of transition-metal complexes.

Based on the Cellular Ligand Field (CLF) model initially developed by Malcolm Gerloch and R. G. Woolley1,2 with further contributions by A. Bencini,3 KESTREL is a user-friendly computer program that performs ligand field calculations in real-time on any monomeric transition metal complex* in any coordination geometry. KESTREL runs on standard desktop computers and laptops. KESTREL takes as its input the 3D structure of a complex. It can then be used in two modes. First, a predictive mode where the metal-ligand bonds are parameterised in terms of their individual σ and π contributions to predict spectroscopic and magnetic properties. Second, an analytical mode that takes spectroscopic and magnetic properties to determine the strength and nature of individual metal-ligand interactions.

KESTREL can also be used for teaching how electronic energies and d-orbital splitting vary with ligand type and coordination geometry. Datasets including input and output files for standard coordination geometries are given here for this purpose.

* KESTREL assumes minimal metal–ligand spatial overlap, an approximation that is appropriate for most 3d transition-metal complexes. Strongly covalent ligands (i.e. CO or CN-) as well as many 4d and 5d metal complexes can still be treated but may yield less reliable results.

KESTREL can be downloaded free-of-charge as an executable file. KESTREL utilises a GUI (Graphical User Interface) that provides a user-friendly interface. Alternatively, a Python package (KestrelPy) is available for advanced use via GitHub.

Features

Multi-configurational energies

Calculates the multi-configurational nature, energy and spin multiplicity of the ground state and each excited state. Tanabe-Sugano-style diagrams can also be generated to visualise how the ligand field affects the electronic states.

UV/CD/MCD spectral simulation

Calculates d-d transition intensities, enabling simulation of ligand-field (d-d) spectra for absorption (UV), circular dichroism (CD) of chiral complexes, and magnetic circular dichroism (MCD) - including A, B and C terms.

EPR A and g values

Calculates EPR parameters including electronic g factors, metal-hyperfine coupling A values and its isotropic and anisotropic contributions.

Real-time calculations

KESTREL effectively calculates in real-time, allowing rapid exploration of electronic structure with ligand field strength.

Paramagnetic susceptibilities

Calculates the variation of magnetic susceptibility with temperature and can model phenomena such as spin-crossover.

d-orbital energies & compositions

Computes the energy of each d-orbital and its orbital character, permitting comparison with experimental and theoretical crystal field splittings.

Parameter Values

KESTREL uses e parameters to describe the bonding interactions between a metal centre and its ligands. Each e parameter describes the type and strength of a bond making each parameter chemically meaningful. The most common e parameters are eσ and eπ which describe the strength of σ and π bonding respectively.

eσ

(eσ) quantifies the strength of ligand-metal σ bonding interactions and values are usually positive to represent σ donation. Typical values range from 3000 cm-1 to ~10000 cm-1 depending on the ligand's donor strength, with stronger field ligands giving larger eσ values.

eπ

(eπx and eπy) quantify the strength of ligand-metal π bonding interactions, with eπx describing the ligand's x direction and eπy the y direction. Values of eπ can be positive or negative to reflect π-donor or π-acceptor ligands. Typical values range from -3000 cm-1 to ~+3000 cm-1.

Spin-Orbit Coupling Constant — ζ

The free-ion spin-orbit coupling constant (ζ) quantifies the coupling between an electron's spin and orbital angular momentum. Values increase across the period and with increasing oxidation state.

KESTREL typically uses the free-ion value which is then reduced by an orbital reduction factor (kiso)

Free-ion Racah Parameters — B and C

The Racah parameters B and C describe the inter-electronic repulsion within the metal d-shell. The ratio C/B ≈ 4 is assumed for most 3d metals. For d1/d9 configurations, Racah values can be set to 0 cm-1.
